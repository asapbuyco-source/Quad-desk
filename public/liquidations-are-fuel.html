Liquidations are fuel, not a signal

A liquidation is a forced closure. When margin runs out, the venue closes the position at market. That order does not care about your view — it adds volume in the direction of the move.

This is why liquidation clusters matter. They show where forced selling or buying is likely to appear if price reaches a level. They are fuel. They are not a schedule.

What the tape shows

Our liquidation board groups executed forced orders by price and by hour, separated into long liquidations (forced sells) and short liquidations (forced buys). A cluster at a price is a record of what happened there — not a guarantee it happens again.

The 24-hour heat strip is the fastest read: red bars are longs being closed, green bars are shorts being closed. A burst of red into a falling price is the signature of a long flush.

What it does not show

  • Timing. Price can sit above a cluster for days.
  • Size beyond the window. Our archive is rolling 24 hours; older clusters are gone.
  • Hidden leverage. Positions collateralised elsewhere are not visible in the print.
A liquidation print is evidence of pain, not a promise of continuation.

How to use it

Pair liquidations with open interest and positioning. Rising OI into a cluster means more fuel. Falling OI after a flush means the fuel is being used. If price reaches a cluster and nothing accelerates, the cluster was smaller than it looked.
