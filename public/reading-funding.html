Reading funding without overreacting

Funding is the periodic payment between longs and shorts on a perpetual future. It exists to keep the perp close to spot. That is the whole mechanism — and it is why funding is better read as a cost of positioning than as a directional forecast.

Start with the interval

Most venues settle every 8 hours. A rate of 0.01% per interval is roughly 0.03% per day, or about 11% annualised. A rate of 0.1% per interval is roughly 110% annualised — a very different statement about who is paying to hold a position.

Look at the interval printed next to the rate. A number without its interval is not comparable across venues.

Then look at the accumulation

One hot settlement can be noise. Seven days of accumulated funding tells you whether longs have been paying persistently. Persistent positive funding with flat price is a warning: one side is paying for a move that is not happening.

Compare venues

If one venue is paying far more than another, capital is crowding there. That can be an arbitrage (long the cheap venue, short the expensive one) or a signal that the expensive venue is about to mean-revert. It is rarely a reason to take a naked directional position.

What funding does not tell you

  • It does not tell you when a crowded position unwinds.
  • It does not include the cost of the hedge, fees or slippage.
  • It does not replace open interest: funding is the price of leverage, OI is how much leverage exists.
Funding tells you who is paying to stay in the trade. It does not tell you who will be forced out first.

Open the terminal and read funding alongside open interest and positioning. If a panel is stale or unavailable, it will say so.
